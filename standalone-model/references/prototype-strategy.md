# Prototype Strategy

## Purpose

Use this reference when deciding whether a prototype is the right next artifact, what it must demonstrate, or how much fidelity it needs. The prototype is an instrument for a decision, not evidence by itself.

## Start with the decision

Before choosing fidelity, state:

1. the decision the prototype must inform;
2. the riskiest assumption it must expose;
3. the behaviour or reaction that would support or challenge that assumption;
4. the audience who must interact with or review it;
5. what the prototype deliberately will not represent.

If no decision or assumption can be named, more artifact fidelity is unlikely to help.

## Choose the lowest sufficient fidelity

- Use a rough structure when testing information order, concept comprehension, or flow shape.
- Use a clickable wireframe when navigation, sequencing, disclosure, or recovery must be experienced.
- Use a stateful interactive prototype when timing, conditional behaviour, data entry, or repeated actions are load-bearing.
- Use higher visual fidelity only when visual trust, brand interpretation, perceived quality, or detailed motion is itself the question.
- Use a coded prototype only when browser, device, input, performance, or implementation behaviour cannot be represented responsibly another way.

Keep alternatives at comparable fidelity when they will be compared. Unequal polish contaminates the decision.

## Scope the artifact

Prototype the shortest path that exposes the risk, plus the material alternate, error, cancellation, or recovery states needed to understand it. Do not reproduce the whole product to make the artifact feel complete.

For a requested interactive Dopamine wireframe, retain that contract while limiting screens, states, motion, and visual detail to what the decision requires.

## Guardrails

- Do not use a polished prototype as proof of usability or desirability.
- Do not increase fidelity because the audience is senior; explain the artifact boundary instead.
- Do not prototype settled, low-risk portions while leaving the load-bearing uncertainty static.
- Design prototypes to be revisable or disposable.
