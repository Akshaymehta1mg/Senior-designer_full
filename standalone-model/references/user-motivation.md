# User Motivation

## Purpose

Use this reference only when the design decision materially depends on why the user is trying to make progress. It sharpens the working user situation; it does not manufacture a persona or replace observed behaviour.

## Motivation dimensions

- **Functional progress:** what the person is trying to accomplish or change in the situation.
- **Emotional progress:** how the person wants to feel, or what feeling they are trying to avoid, when evidence supports it.
- **Social progress:** how the outcome affects how the person is seen, supported, trusted, or coordinated with others, when relevant and supported.

For each dimension, record the source and confidence label. A dimension may remain unknown or irrelevant.

## Framing pattern

Describe the situation and desired progress without embedding a solution:

> When [supported situation or trigger], the person is trying to [supported progress], because [supported consequence or motivation]. Today they [current behaviour], but [breakdown] prevents or weakens that progress.

Treat this as an editable hypothesis. Invite correction when inferred material affects the frame.

## Guardrails

- Prefer recent, concrete behaviour over stated hypothetical preference.
- Do not infer emotion from a screen problem, demographic label, or product category.
- Do not treat a requested feature as the user's underlying job.
- Do not fill all three dimensions for completeness.
- Keep the articulation open to more than one solution direction.
